Circumstances have led me to undergo another moving process. Beyond the delights of living among boxes and the difficulties moving entails, I want to take something positive from the moment. Learn, since we are at it.

They say moving is one of the most stressful situations one can experience. Personally, I've never found it to be that bad. I suppose I've been lucky the times I've moved and haven't had to overcome too many problems.

One of the first things I realize is how little we actually need. You have everything in boxes, and in the last few days, you barely live with a toothbrush and some indispensable items. The rest? Boxed up.

Wow, the world doesn't seem to end, and you don't have too many things around you. If you add to that the fact that half of the things you've packed, you didn't even remember existed... The harsh reality is that we are machines for accumulating and filling space. As if having more would make us more or better.

Then begins the purge. It's one of the few fun things (in my opinion) about moving. The pleasure of filling bags and boxes with useless things and letting go of what ties you down. Because let's not kid ourselves, what we have ends up possessing us; I find it pleasant to free myself from its grasp and feel lighter. In the three times I've moved and gotten rid of more than half of my things, I've had the thought that my life comes with me, that it has nothing to do with everything packed in boxes, and that my home will be where I hang my hat.

That feeling doesn't last very long, to be honest. After a short time, I go back to jealously guarding my prized possessions as if my life depended on it. But after several experiences, it doesn't worry me too much because I know the moment of letting go will come again.

And so is life, letting go of what surrounds you. Shedding the old to embrace the new, without fear, without needs. Letting go doesn't mean having nothing or being alone, not at all. It means that, little or much, what you have around you will be just what should be around you. No more, no less. The rest is excess, weighing down our bags.

It's very curious how my feelings about the place I leave behind have changed. At first, I felt very sad to leave it, even knowing that the new place was much better. But you grow fond of it, you’ve spent a lot of time there, and you have that kind of Stockholm syndrome that puts you in the state of "better the devil you know...". That's the dangerous point, the moment of fear that prevents you from trying new things and embarking on new adventures.

As the process progressed, I let go of that feeling of attachment to the devil I knew and started to develop a new, unknown, and exciting feeling about the good things to come. To the point that now, in a surprisingly short period, I no longer like the old place. I understand the affection I had for it, but it becomes clear that I no longer belong there. It's very obvious now.

In the end, it’s about overcoming that moment of fear, closing your eyes, gritting your teeth, and moving forward. A few steps in, you see that the new isn't so bad, that the future is never as uncertain, and that there are great things waiting for you out there. Like walking through a thick fog, we can't expect to have any guarantees about what will appear out there; but for that very reason, why think that what awaits is bad or worse than what we already have?
