You may or may not now about Paul Lutus. If you don't, you have the opportunity of getting inspired by the story of a brilliant person; if you do, check out if you share my own conclusions about his story.

# Early days

Paul Lutus is a famous programmer that is mainly know by creating the word processor software <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Writer" target="_blank" alt="Apple Writer">Apple Writer.</a> But his story goes far beyond that. He had a rough childhood and left home as soon as he could and ended up on the street.

There was a time when it was possible to live of the streets in California doing jobs here and there. The hippie vibe was starting and people weren't afraid to engage with someone _"living on the street"_. He made a few bucks as an electrician and voice got around that he was really good. That way he gains some reputation and made his way designing circuits and electronics for NASA. Yes, for NASA.

They were looking for a circuit design for the lights of the space shuttle that were able to dim as expected. He showed up at the interview with no credentials and no formal studies but with a piece of paper with a design in it. He was hired right away.

He began fiddling with HP calculators that were among the first that were programmable. He wrote a program regarding the planets movements to figure out the planets positions given a few input values, it was a model of the solar system. Another one was a game where you would draw on a piece of graph paper, you'd draw an earth and a moon. And you'd put in numerical values in the calculator program, and you'd have the spacecraft take off step-by-step, and you make little dots on the graph paper. He published some programs on magazines and that last one cough the attention of <a href="https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/" target="_blank" alt="Jet Propulsion Laboratory">Jet Propulsion Laboratory.</a> The Jet Propulsion Laboratory is based out of Caltech University, and they designed the current Mars robot, Perseverance, for example.

![Perseverance Mars rover](/assets/images/paulLutusASuccessCase/perseverance.png#postImageMedium)

He started to make some money working for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and as soon as he had enough, he quit. He actually "retired". He had enough to buy a piece of land in Oregon and build a wood cabin. He hated living in the city and paying rent. So he completely changed his life and went from working at an office building to be alone in the woods without electricity.

# The woods

Once alone in the woods, he continued to write programs for the HP calculator and publishing articles from time to time on scientific magazines. He quickly got bored with the calculator and needed something else to stimulate his imagination. He saw an add for the new Apple II computer on a local newspaper and decided to buy one.

When the item arrived he quickly thought he had made a big mistake spending almost all of his savings on this machine. The computer needed electricity to work and his technology items at the time where a stove and an axe.

Since the computer was there... he asked for a construction permit to improve the cabin. With this permit, they provide a single power outlet, so you can plug the power tools and such. He constructed nothing but deployed a 1200-foot extension chord from that outlet to his cabin in order to power up the computer.

The machine came with <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC" target="_blank" alt="BASIC programming language">BASIC programming language</a> installed. He tried it a little but found it very slow and abandoned it quickly. He then moved to Assembly and keep writing code for the computer.

Some magazine asked him to write an article about Einstein's theory of relativity. He was working on it and ended up with several chunks of text for the article. He had doubts of how to order the different parts, so he wrote them on pieces of paper and start moving them around, like a puzzle to see how they looked together in a certain order. He had his light bulb moment... _"wait a minute, maybe I can write a program in my new computer to do that job"_. He wrote a program to move around text on the screen, sound familiar? Yes, a word processor.

The article went a little better but the program improved **a lot** since he was adding features as he needed them. Apple heard about that program and bought it from him. He started then a relationship with Apple and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Writer" target="_blank" alt="Apple Writer">Apple Writer</a> was born.

Eventually, Apple asked him for improvements and new features as the users got feedback. This time Paul didn't sold them the new version but asked them for royalties. This was a good deal for Apple because they didn't have available cash since they were on their early stages. Paul asked for a 25% royalty fee, which is... a lot, but nobody knew at that time. Apple accepted without hesitation.

The program sold like crazy and Paul Lutus never had financial problems again.

# The childhood he never had

He used the money to move to another piece of land and buy a proper house; his "improved" cabin. But kept living the same simple lifestyle... without fancy toys or extravagant life choices. Then he could do what he wanted to do since always: **play.**

He sails on his boat to Alaska every year since 2002. He loves nature and science, he writes articles and programs about Android, graphics, drones, hardware, Linux, mathematics, data bases, planetary science, programming, psychology, Python, Raspberry and that's just to mention a few from his website. So, what he basically does is what any technology passionate person would do if he or she could afford it.

Now he can expend his time playing. Finally.

# Where there is a will there's a way

There are a couple of videos on his YouTube channel that I think define his way of thinking and behaving.

He likes drones. So he bought one and learnt everything about flying drones. He crashed it on a tree, so what does he do? He buys another one and goes looking for the original one. Finally, he finds it stuck on a tree branch. Now he knows where it is and discovers that he can actually see it from his porch with a pair of binnacles. And of course... all hell brakes loose.

He can see the machine stuck on that branch every day. It is a good piece of equipment, and it doesn't look damaged, so it is appropriate to recover it. But of course, he would need to climb a tree to recover it. What does he do? He learns how to climb trees, of course.

He spent a long period of time practicing and learning until he was confident enough to try and retrieve the missing drone. And he did, of course.

# The rational skeptic

Same way with the recent COVID19 pandemic. He wrote a series of programs that implemented pandemic prediction formulas. With time, he developed a complex mathematical model to assess how different strategies could affect the infection ratio.

He saw with his own eyes, how the measures proposed (social distance, hygiene, quarantine periods) were correct. He made that decision with his own research in front of him.

Learning about epidemiology, reading science papers, researching the correct formulas... it's very hard work. It is always worth it as it is a way to understand for himself the world around us and that's a way of life.

# My take-out

I felt very inspired by his story. These are the two main conclusions that I reach:

1. Formal studies and universities are a very valid option, but it is not exclusive to have a very lucrative career. We all know some celebrities on the field that don't have any computer science degree, and they have done very well. I still think that if you have the opportunity, by all means, attend college. But I like to see how it is not an exclusive field. I wouldn't like to see brilliant people left behind because of lack of some degree.
2. Learning your own way it's always a good choice. What if you don't have any idea on how to climb trees? You go and learn. There is no end of what you can achieve by learning, mostly on these days.
