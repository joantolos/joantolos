They seem to, but they don't laugh. They emit a high-pitched guttural sound reminiscent of human laughter, and with their disheveled appearance, they can appear friendly animals. But they are not.

They are scavengers that attack at night, treacherously and in packs. They target the young, the weak, and the sick. Without compassion or remorse, they move on to their next victim with the sole purpose of filling their bellies.

But they seem to laugh and seduce us, dazzle us with their large, deep eyes, pretending to deceive us with their cunning ways.

The law of the jungle has fallen onto the asphalt. One can aspire to be as noble and firm as a lion or as sleek and swift as a hawk, but when night falls, the hyenas lurk with their sticky laughter. Taking advantage of the fallen, the weak, dirty scavengers making their false laughter echo.

During the day, they camouflage themselves, blend in with the rest of the honorable animals, but they gather in corners to conspire. Choosing their next victim. They are animals, but if you observe their eyes for a moment, you can identify the gaze of those who conspire. They pretend to be part of the pack, but at night, their eyes dilate their pupils to the maximum, revealing their true sickly, empty, and bloodthirsty gaze. They are the psychopaths of the animal kingdom.

I fear they have expanded their borders and mutated with great skill. They still hide during the day, behind a tie or a conciliatory smile. But they remain just as ruthless and will exercise their nocturnal power at the slightest opportunity we give them. They stroll through a circus where the maximum penalty is death and there is no safety net or tamer. The rest of us try to perform somersaults without a safety net, holding onto each other so as not to fall while the lazy hyenas spend their hours sprawled carelessly on the ground, with the confidence and self-sufficiency of those who know that sooner or later you will fall. And then you will have two options: you can join their heartless clan and become one of them, or you can fight to reach a helping hand that will lift you back up to the heights, or worst-case scenario, fall prey to the pack.

I hope that a helping hand will be offered to save me and pull me out of danger, and also to slap me the day I start resembling the hyenas too much. The day when filling my belly and my pockets becomes more important than the people I choose to surround myself with. That day, when I enter their fold, their pack of despicable beings, I will once again pray to be protected from having power over the tears and laughter of others. I will think of all those who have flown far away and saved themselves from the hyenas one day, one month, one year more. I will not forget their thoughts and teachings; I will want to be a cheetah, tiger, panther, never a scavenger. I will strive not to be just another one within the walls of the zoo they have built around us, where they keep us oppressed. I will spread my wings, fly, breathe pure air, and rid my lungs of the stale smell of suffering and sorrow exuded by the hyenas around me.

In the jungle book, the animals were never like this.
