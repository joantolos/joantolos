Making a decision that radically changes your life is always something frightening. It’s disheartening to see how accustomed we are to routine and how altering it terrifies us to varying degrees. I’m referring to decisions like changing your place of residence, changing jobs, or ending a relationship... Transcendent decisions.

The reality is that any decision involves a risk, and we make them hundreds of times a day. In fact, there are studies that show that a decision is made a few milliseconds before our conscious brain turns it into reality. Are we then doomed to follow what our subconscious has already decided for us?

Putting pseudo-scientific questions aside, it’s clear that we mull over things a lot before doing something like this, hoping to have a more formed opinion, to find a way to ensure success, as if that were possible. The outcome will be what it will be, and although it’s good to analyze and plan, in the end, things will always turn out as they should, and these decisions don’t necessarily have long-term implications.

That is to say, you can make many mistakes in countless different things, but if you do what you must, in the end, all the pieces will fit into place, without exception. It’s a matter of time: if you are more fortunate in making decisions, you might gain some time to become what you can be.

On the other hand, if you dabble a bit here and there without fully committing to anything, it’s difficult to reach any defined destination. You simply bounce around while the universe conspires to keep you still and finally bring order to chaos. Because that’s what it’s all about in the end. Our existence is the result of a delicate balance that nature constantly adjusts. Yet, instead of respecting its laws and going with its flow, we insist on swimming upstream, against the current, with aching muscles, cursing our surroundings. When the only thing we know for sure is that no matter how much we swim, run, and kick, we will all eventually end up in the sea.

It’s up to us whether our path is straight, direct, joyless, and without grace to reach the sea as quickly as possible, or whether we use the tools at our disposal to make our journey long, interesting, and enriching.

Our tools may seem limited; it seems like we wake up in this world with little more than our hands, and so it is. The truth is, we don’t need much more.

It’s up to us to choose wisely or poorly in our daily decisions that will lead us down one path or another. The secret is that all paths are good. They all have something to teach us, and we have something to contribute to those who come after us.

However, to walk this path, we must take risks, make decisions, make mistakes, fall, get up, and face the consequences of our actions. In short: be brave.

I’ve always thought that the bravest person is not the one who sticks their head into a lion’s mouth, jumps off a cliff, or plays with sharks. The bravest person is the one who gets up every day at seven in the morning to support their family, the one who knows what needs to be done and does it, even if there are things they don’t feel like doing. Because only with absolute commitment to your plan can you achieve excellence. Design a plan (good or bad), be consistent and tenacious (if not stubborn), and achieve it.

This implies having to live through a few unpleasant farewells along the way. Again, you have to be brave to say goodbye. A lot.

Being brave is the opposite of choosing the easy path. It’s doing what must be done.

Avoiding a farewell is always easier than going through with it, which is why we often continue doing what we do because we don’t like saying goodbye. Letting go of what we have, as if that defines who we are. But it’s not like that; we are who we are, and it has nothing to do with what surrounds us, which is completely circumstantial.

Maturing is learning to let go. Truly mature people are those who have learned to live with goodbyes in their lives. Those who have managed to accept that people and things come and go from our lives, that we benefit from them for a moment, and then we move on. Only in this way will we acquire the maturity necessary to make the final decision without fear, to ultimately let go of the only thing we truly have: our own life.

So, we have several years ahead (many, with luck) to train and practice. Be brave and learn to let go. This knowledge will be indispensable for our personal growth and will help us become the best version of ourselves.

Because when you do what you want, asking yourself at every step if what you’re doing is really what you want to do, life automatically brings you closer to those people you can learn from and distances you from those who do not contribute anything.

In this case, the only conscious decision is: "I’m going to do what I want to do."

It sounds very easy, doesn’t it? So why don’t we do it then? First, because it’s not as easy as it seems to know what you want, and second and most importantly: because it’s scary. Making decisions terrifies us even though everything, from our body, our brain to the circumstances around us, constantly pushes us to make those decisions. Waiting for us to say out loud what move we want to make with the cards we’ve been dealt.

On the contrary, many choose to postpone the decision and prolong it in an agony that leads nowhere. Then many people wonder why they’re not happy and why "everything goes wrong." And the answer in most cases is the same: be brave, make decisions, and face farewells—they don’t bite.
