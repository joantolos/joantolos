A couple of weeks ago, I had to stay late at work. One of my colleagues was driving down to Barcelona and offered to give me a ride. It was early morning, and we were both very tired and sleepy. I found it quite hard to keep my eyes open. As my body settled into the soft seat, it was like a switch deactivated inside me, shifting from work mode to rest mode.

The music played softly, pleasant, like a subtle background melody accompanying our thoughts. At that moment, I was thinking about my home, my girl, my mouse, my bed... After so many consecutive hours working, your mind starts to dry up of creativity, freshness, imagination, efficiency—in short, of everything secondary, leaving only the essential, the vital. Those thoughts were my essentials. Just ten minutes ago, I was focused on work details, trying to concentrate to squeeze out the last drops of coherent thought left in me. Ten minutes later, none of that mattered, not even why I had stayed to work so late.

When we turned onto La Meridiana, we encountered one of those sequences of traffic lights that I've only seen in Barcelona, where you can't pass through two green lights in a row and are forced to stop at each one. That's how we proceeded, stopped at a traffic light, me staring fixedly at the red light, waiting and counting the seconds for it to turn green. Eventually, my colleague driving gave up on the cruelty of the traffic lights and stopped trying to catch a long stretch in green. Instead, when he started moving, he did so slowly, gradually gliding to the next traffic light, which had already turned red. And so, accepting the circumstances, the journey began to feel more pleasant. We became aware of the music, hummed and whistled a bit... I observed the people on the street, their expressions and attitudes. Both of us sunk a little deeper into our seats and smiled a bit inside.

We got tired of waiting for the green light from the traffic lights that didn't allow us to see what was happening around us.

Lately, I've found myself in these circumstances: waiting for my green light to be able to cover a few kilometers straight and upwards, always upwards. I've seen myself with my eyes too fixed on the red light, getting tense because it doesn't change, cursing the traffic light controllers, the city, and the circumstances. All the time I've spent staring at the traffic light, I've missed the streets and their people, the music, and the good company. Life is everything that happens while you're waiting for something to happen, and the only thing that happens is life and time.

I took the initiative and wanted to stop looking at the red light, consuming myself inside, and instead, I focused my gaze on the road. I wanted to focus on the goal and not on the circumstances, on the path that will take me to my destination.

By changing my perspective, my traffic light turned green without me even noticing it. I continued to move forward with my gaze fixed on the horizon, as if it were the most natural thing in the world—and indeed, it is. With my eyes fixed on the road, I could glance sideways at my old hated traffic lights that delayed me so much, and now their lights seem to wink at me, as if they wanted to tell me that I finally understood their purpose, which is to enrich me and not to hinder me as I initially thought.

The pause at this last traffic light has been long; I hope I haven't missed too much of what was happening around me. In any case, it's green now, and I've been enjoying it for a couple of days. But I won't make the same mistake of rushing to try to beat time, instead I'll start slowly and look around me, stopping obsessing over traffic lights and radars that are there to do their job and not to make me miserable.

The next red light will come, undoubtedly, I'll stop calmly, look around me, enjoy the music, the colors the light creates on the windshield, the smells, and the laughter. I'll take the pause for what it is: an opportunity to become aware of where I am and what I am. When I find myself obsessed again with my eyes fixed on the red light, I'll look back at the road, the traffic light will turn green again, and it will be time to leave. To search for new journeys where I might end up on the ground only to get up again.

The next day, after getting out of the shower early to go back to work, I crossed paths with my girl entering the bathroom, and she said, "Well, see you tomorrow, right?" Assuming that night I would also arrive home late from work. It was at that moment that I realized my traffic light had turned green and that I was ready to move forward.

That's the plan I outlined that early morning returning from working somewhere, doing something. And I'm sure that in ten years, I won't remember how much the company invoiced in 2013, but I will remember my wife's slow and steady breathing in bed, the day I arrived home late from work.

Since then, I make plans to be surprised. I try to put myself in situations where there's a good margin of improvisation and let circumstances surprise and enrich me.

I look at the traffic lights on my path for what they are: tools to prevent us from cracking our heads open and never again as enemies.
