The other day, I was watching a superhero movie at home. I watched it alone with headphones to avoid waking my wife, who was peacefully sleeping in the next room.

At first, I put it on just to pass the time, checking my emails while it played in the background, thinking about my own things. Soon enough, the action on the screen caught my attention. I paused for a moment to prepare a snack and got ready to enjoy the movie, but not before slightly opening the bedroom door to watch my wife's rhythmic breathing under the sheets for a few seconds. Knowing she is well always brings me a sense of calm and peace that I greatly enjoy.

Fully attentive, I let myself be carried away by the fantasy story. As the movie progressed, I managed to forget my daily routine, being completely absorbed by the screen for a long while. I got so involved in the plot that towards the end, I began to feel a familiar sensation, a visceral emotion buried over the years. The feeling was familiar yet strange, like running into an old friend you know but can't quite remember from where.

Gradually, the sensation became clearer, and I recognized myself as a child, watching old movies my father would play, where a charismatic protagonist captivated me. I vividly remembered how, back then, I used my imagination to become that character.

I embodied my favorite characters, ones that I now realize I didn't even fully understand but were irresistibly charismatic. With them, I could be anything, my imagination taking me to unknown, exciting, and wonderful places.

Moreover, nothing was permanent; tomorrow, I could be something else or everything at once.

That Sunday afternoon, upon recognizing this familiar and pleasant sensation, I decided to give in to it and let myself be carried away. And yes, there I was, becoming a character from the screen, shouldering the responsibility of saving the world from catastrophic calamities. I ran, fought, and suffered with him. I was a child once again.

I now realize that these childhood impersonations, that excessive use of imagination, have partly made me who I am. Not because of any intellectual benefit it might have had then, but because of the passion with which I did it.

I played at being characters, building and destroying, living terrifying, fantastic, romantic, and dangerous situations. That passion I employed shaped my character from a very young age.

Reconnecting with that sensation was extremely pleasant, and when the movie ended, I felt happy, complete, and fulfilled. The funniest part is that I had seen that movie before, so it wasn't even the element of surprise.

I felt the urge to lock myself in my room, like when I was a child, to dress up and recreate my favorite scenes again.

Well, things have changed. My room is no longer just my room. It's an adult bedroom I share with the woman I love. I wouldn't change that for anything, but I also won't give up my newly rediscovered ability to be whoever I want to be.

From that Sunday, I learned that it was very useful back then to let myself go to the fullest, enjoying every minute of my games with passion. That attitude hasn't stopped being useful just because the years have passed.

I may be more mature, more domesticated... But investing passion in my activities will continue to bring good things.

I'm very satisfied with how I've directed my life so far, and circumstances have led me to rekindle feelings from my childhood that Sunday afternoon.

I plan to take note and apply that mindset in my daily life. I'll keep maturing, but I'll play much more. I'll lock myself in my room, wear capes and masks, possess wonderful powers and abilities. I'll believe myself to be big, small, more or less talented.

We are and always remain that child. He had the power to be whoever he wanted to be, and I placed obstacles in his way. Now it's time to give him one of the reins.
