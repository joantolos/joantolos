The way we communicate has changed dramatically over the years. We have gone from oral tradition to writing, then to printed press, then to the radio, then to the TV, and now we are using social media. Each of these steps has made it easier for us to communicate with each other. Social media has made it possible for us to communicate with people all over the world instantly.

Most of the people get their news exclusively from social media. This is a very dangerous trend because social media is not a reliable source of information. The information on social media is often biased and inaccurate.

Nothing can be completely explained on 214 characters, there is no place for context, background or nuance. This is why we need to be careful about the news we consume on social media and make sure to cross-check the information with other sources.

The journalist writing for social media, don't necessarily have the best interest of the reader in mind. They are often more concerned with getting clicks and likes, than with providing accurate information. They want to write in a way that satisfies their audiences, not in a way that provides accurate information. Meanwhile, the truth gets more and more obfuscated. This is creating big silos of information where confirmation bias is the norm.

It is important to be aware of these trends and to be critical of the information we consume on social media.

# Like bees to honey

The headlines that generate more clicks are often the ones that are the most sensational. This is because people are attracted to drama and conflict. The media knows this and they often use headlines that are designed to generate clicks, even if the information in the article is not accurate.

Nuances explanations and tones of grey are often lost in the noise of social media. Everything is black and white. People are attracted to clear and simple messages, even if they are not accurate. We are getting used to reach for simple solutions to complex problems. And we fall into the trap, again and again, like bees to honey.

The most rational first thought when dealing with a complex issue usually is "it depends" but this is not the kind of answer that people want to hear. They want a simple answer that they can share on social media and feel good about. That answer is not sexy, it doesn't generate clicks so it is usually disregarded.

If I write an article about Brexit, for example, and I say that it is a complex issue and that there is no simple answer, that article is not going to generate as many clicks as an article that has a clear and simple message like: _"Brexit is a mistake, we should have stayed in the EU"_ or _"Brexit is the best thing that happened to the UK on the last hundred years"_, I bet I am going to obtain a huge amount of clicks. But, what is a rational take on Brexit? Is Brexit good or bad? Well... **it depends.**

But it looks like nobody wants to explore that possibility and figure out what that _"depends"_ entails. But the truth lies somewhere inside that _"depends"._

# Lies for clicks

There is even a worse situation where the writer is lying deliberately just for shock value. I want to explain a couple of instances that I have stumbled upon in my social media feed.

One of them involved the treatment of patience on a hospital in Spain. The post claimed that they witnesses a patience collapse at the emergency room and stayed lying on the floor for 45 minutes because nobody was available or willing to attend him. Several health workers for that same hospital quickly responded that this is impossible, never happened and simply: a lie. All of them where blocked by the author, no explanation, no debate, nothing. Just clicks and shares.

The other instance was about a supposed "Muslim ban" in the United States. The headline said: "Trump bans all Muslims from entering the United States". When I clicked on the article I found out that the ban only applied to citizens of 7 countries, none of them being Muslim majority countries.

In both cases the headlines were designed to generate clicks by playing on people's fears. But the information in the articles was not accurate.

The problem is that people often don't read the whole article, they just read the headline and share it on social media

# Quick and easy

I wrote about this quick fix society that we are creating before, you can read about it <a href="https://www.joantolos.com/blog/the-quick-fix-society" target="_blank" alt="The quick fix society">here.</a> You can see that trend clearly exposed even on the way the YouTube and TikTok videos are edited. Everything is extremely quick and easy to consume. The rhythm is frenetic, the more you can fit on less time the better.

There are a whole genre of videos kind of "do it yourself" where you can see people doing things that you would never dream of doing yourself. From baking pies, to building a log cabin. They make it look so easy that you think: "Hey, I can do that too!". And then you try and you realise that it is not that easy after all.

Our brain does not differentiate between fact and fiction, so in a way we are being tricked into thinking that we can do anything, just because we saw somebody doing it on a video. This is creating a false sense of reality and it is not good for our mental health.  Also, since our brain takes that "new ability" for granted, it is not going to bother to learn it, because, in a way, we think we already know it. You get a little bit of accomplishment feeling just for watching the video. So we get a false sense of accomplishment that is not going to help us in the future when we actually need to know how to do that thing. It destroy our drive to get up of the coach and do stuff because for our brain, we are kind of doing things constantly.

# Are we loosing abilities little by little?

![Addiction to social media](/assets/images/communicationBreakdown/social.png#postImageBig)

When human beings moved from oral tradition to writing, we lost the capacity of remember long stories. There is no need anymore, we can write it down so we don't have to remember, that is awesome, but know we have less memory. When we went from radio to TV, we lost the possibility of imagine the story in our heads, there is no need anymore because the image on the TV does the job for us, we have now less imagination. Going from that to social media is affecting our capacity of attention.

Quick videos, short headlines, even shorter articles are diminish our ability to pay attention to a single thing without interruptions. US presidential debates used to last for hours, with single interventions timing up to 45 minutes. There were intermissions just to get dinner and then come back to the debate. Nuance, details, context, background were included and discussed. Now we are lucky if we are able to hear a candidate talk for 20 minutes straight without interruptions. And even then, you will have several headlines running on the bottom of the screen so you can read while trying to listen. Or even worse, a little screen on the corner of the screen with the face of the journalist that is "analysing" what the candidate is saying. So you are trying to listen to the candidate and read what the journalist is saying about what the candidate is saying.

What will happen a hundred years from now? Are we going to be able to pay attention for more than a few minutes?

# What if it already happened

There is a very famous experiment about a frog in a pot of boiling water. The story goes that if you put a frog in a pot of boiling water it will jump out right away, but if you put it in a pot of cold water and you start heating it up slowly, the frog will not notice the change in temperature and it will stay in the water until it boils to death.

The story is not true, but the experiment is. So what does this have to do with us? We are like that frog, we are not going to notice the changes in our abilities until they are gone forever.

We think of those outcomes with fear, because we think is something that is going to happen in the future, but what if it already happened? What if we are already living in a world where ancient abilities are no longer with us?

# Original wisdom

Not so long ago I read this wonderful book that I can't recommend enough: <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/328149.Original_Wisdom" target="_blank" alt="Original Wisdom: Stories of an Ancient Way of Knowing by Robert Wolff">Original Wisdom: Stories of an Ancient Way of Knowing by Robert Wolff</a>. The book is about the author's experiences learning and living with the San Bushmen in the Kalahari desert.

![Original Wisdom book cover](/assets/images/communicationBreakdown/original-wisdom.jpeg#postImageExtraSmall)

The San Bushmen are one of the oldest human cultures on earth. They have been living in the Kalahari desert for over 20,000 years. They are one of the few cultures that have managed to preserve their ancient way of life.

One of the things that I found the most fascinating about the book is the way the San Bushmen think about time. For them, time is not linear, it is cyclical. The past, present and future are all happening at the same time. This is a very different way of thinking about time than the one we are used to.

The San Bushmen also have a very different relationship with nature. They see themselves as part of nature, not as separate from it. They have a deep understanding of the natural world and they know how to live in harmony with it.

The author explains one instance where he found the tribe seating together on the floor, apparently doing nothing. He approached to one of the spoke-man of the tribe and ask him what they were doing. The man looked confused and funny as he didn't understand the purpose of the question, he answered:

> -We are sitting

> -Yes, I can see that, but why?

More strange looks...

> -Why do we need a reason to seat?

The author, who is an anthropologist, surely thinks that there has to be a reason why these people reunite together at certain times just to seat there. Are they seeing, hearing or feeling something that we no longer can see, hear or feel? Our senses evolve with our environment and our way of life. It is not beyond the realm of imagination that those people can actually sense something that we can't.

Maybe future generations will ask themselves why we are able to spend hours reading a book and will ask "why" as the anthropologist author of the book. And we will think _"what a silly question, how I am supposed to read otherwise?"_ But maybe the concept of reading is completely alien to them.

Or even talking... _"what are those noises you do with your throat?"_

# Mob over individual

The individual is lost more and more in favour of the group. This is a trend that is happening all over the world. We are becoming more and more like a hive mind. Who writes Wikipedia? Well, nobody... and everybody at the same time.

Is that good or bad? Well, again going to the beginning of the post... **it depends.** Yes! I am not going to get any clicks, but it is really what I think.
