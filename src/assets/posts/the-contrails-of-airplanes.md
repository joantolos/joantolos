Some mornings, while I'm on the bus to work, I look up at the sky and find one of those radiant blues. Those skies that if you stare at them without seeing anything but blue, you can even feel dizzy. The buildings are silhouetted against the background, revealing the unnaturalness of their shapes in nature, how little they fit.

If I'm lucky, sometimes I see those unmistakable white contrails left by airplanes as they pass. From what I understand, if certain meteorological conditions are met (details of which I am unaware), it is very likely to see a lot of them throughout the day. Conspiracy theorists use them to concoct theories and government plans to control us, fumigate us, or commit a thousand other atrocities.

Personally, aside from mass destruction, I consider it something beautiful to see when it happens. Like an artificial rainbow, the signature of our passage through the world.

Sometimes at the beginning of the contrail, you can see the plane that creates it, advancing in a way that seems slow but actually isn't. I can't help but think about its microclimate and how thousands of peanuts, neatly packed in their little bags, are passing over our heads at that very moment. What a beautiful miracle of science that allows us to travel from one place to another with our favorite nuts. So far, it is not known that they suffer from the dreaded jet lag; time will tell.

If you look at them closely, they make interesting patterns and curves; they even intertwine. Sometimes they are like lines on a music score waiting for notes, sometimes they are like a child's doodles. It doesn't matter much because the next day, everything will be blue again.

That's our advantage; the next day is always blue. Sometimes the blue will be less pristine, and surely there will be gray and rainy days, but why not give it a try?

I am very fond of routines when they are well executed. I enjoy the pleasure of designing a method and perfecting it. As time goes by and it works thanks to additions and modifications, the satisfaction is greater. But be careful, there is the danger of becoming rigid, of losing flexibility. Of believing that the version is the best possible version.

That's when I think of airplane contrails. Those impertinent lines that insist on spoiling the perfect blue sky, but without them, the day wouldn't be as perfect when it is. I learn to appreciate change, to move with it instead of resisting it. To accept it as part of the process of growth and perfection.

After all, in a few days, it will always, always be blue again.
