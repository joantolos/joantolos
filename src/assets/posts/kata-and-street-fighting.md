There are moments in the day when one has to summon inner strength and show what lies within. Even if just for a moment, a little bit, at least once a day, you have to show your teeth.

The alarm goes off, and I get up a bit groggy to try and exercise first thing in the morning. It wakes me up and gets me ready for a shower. On exercise days, I have a very energetic morning and don’t need coffee.

The morning ritual begins, the ritual of coffee with milk. I like to listen to the radio in the background and feel the world waking up around me. Important things happen in the morning, first thing in the morning. I like feeling part of the collective that gets going early. I think about my coworkers, my bosses, my friends, and acquaintances who, at this moment (give or take half an hour), are all getting ready, preparing for the circus.

Soon we plunge into the sea, surrounded by sharks. We have to come up to the surface occasionally to breathe and feel the sunlight on our skin. Sharks aren’t in a hurry; they don’t need to be. They have big, powerful gills, allowing them to dive slowly, waiting for you to fall. They are the long-distance runners of the deep waters; they know they will get what they want. Only a conspiracy of other sharks against one of them can stop them and devour them alive.

We think we are little fish or seabirds that have to dip their heads underwater because there is no other choice. We need to catch some food to survive, and for that, we have to get a bit wet. Or so we think.

In reality, we are baby sharks pretending not to notice while swimming deeper and deeper, and our lungs are turning into hard, deep gills. We complain and raise a fuss about the deep-sea sharks, cursing them and shaking our fists at them. We speak ill of them behind their backs and curse them. The sharks know this, and not only do they not mind, but they also consider it a fundamental part of the game. They want us agitated and resentful because the more time we spend fighting against the established power of the sharks, the closer we get to them and the closer we are to becoming one of them. When that happens, they welcome you with open arms, and past curses disappear. However, you must be aware that now you are a shark, and the only predator that can challenge you is the one sitting next to you, swimming side by side in the depths. Only they can now dispute your bread and take your future from your mouth. Be careful with them because when they open their mouths wide to attack and swallow the biggest prey, their lidless, expressionless, doll-like eyes reveal their unchanging nature: ruthless predators whose only interest is surviving above all else. The shark always swims alone; it doesn’t belong to a pack. Don’t mistake thinking you belong to a team.

It’s at these moments that I’m glad I’ve had opportunities in my life to practice street fighting, to have to fight for every crumb of bread. It’s not pleasant, but it’s a long-term valuable experience that can get you out of more than one tight spot.

I arrive at my desk, clear my table, sit down, and wake up the computer, ready for a new day. At that moment, everyone wears their kimono and gets ready to warm up with their favorite katas.

A kata is a set of ordered movements that prepare you to solve more complex problems. That is, repeating the formal solution over and over again trains your movements and helps you internalize them correctly. Only by having a large and extensive vocabulary of perfectly executed katas can you navigate the real world in unforeseen situations. Continuing with the analogy, testing your skills in a real fight.

But the real world is ruthless and cruel, with no referees to ensure the safety of the fight or the correct application of movements. Anything goes, and survival of the fittest are the rules, and everything else becomes irrelevant when things get ugly.

It’s then that you have to show your teeth a bit, bite hard, and throw a few low blows, bringing out the street fighting spirit that has developed over the years to deliver a few precise and decisive movements.

I love katas; they are elegant and thoughtful. I don’t like street fighting as much, but I am grateful for it. It has gotten me out of many tight spots by having those reflexes ingrained in me, ignoring what’s proper, forcing the situation, and surprising the opponent with a hard and unexpected blow.
